# M1 race day: horse blurbs for the Card screen

Status: candidates for Joseph's pick. Written 2026-11-13 by the designer for `docs/specs/m1-race-day.md` section 4.1, detail box line 3. The chosen lines move into the card data. This file is not read by the game.

Rules each line meets: one line, 28 characters or fewer counting spaces and punctuation, printable ASCII, straight apostrophe only. Each line is character, not a tip: no prediction, no rating, no advice, nothing about injury, age or being sent away, and no real people, brands or tracks. Each one is true to the horse's name, style or strongest stat.

Counts are in brackets. **Ship** marks the line I would use.

## 1. Gravy Subpoena (stalker, speed 76)

- Sits second, files a motion. [28]
- **Ship:** Served papers to the leader. [28]
- Comes with a side of gravy. [27]

Why: a stalker's job, told as a legal errand. The first line names a running position the race may not give it, which reads like a promise.

## 2. Hold My Oats (front-runner, speed 86)

- Out of the gate like gossip. [28]
- **Ship:** Wants the lead and a snack. [27]
- Breakfast, then the lead. [25]

Why: front-running plus the oats in the name, said as a plain list of wants. It is the least writerly line, which suits a horse whose whole plan is "go".

## 3. Decaf By Mistake (closer, kick 72)

- **Ship:** Wakes up at the far turn. [25]
- Somebody check the coffee. [26]
- Naps early, works late. [23]

Why: it is the closer's style and the name in one image, and it says where the horse comes alive without saying it gets there.

## 4. Legally A Horse (stalker, stamina 78)

- **Ship:** Papers say horse. We agree. [27]
- Notarized, and in no hurry. [27]
- Has a lawyer. Has stamina. [26]

Why: deadpan confirmation, a different shape from the two other legal names on the card. It is affectionate, and the joke is on the paperwork, not the horse.

## 5. Left On Read (closer, kick 74)

- Replies late. Very late. [24]
- **Ship:** Typing... still typing... [25]
- Saw your message. Busy. [23]

Why: a visual joke, the only one of the eight, and it is the closer's late run as a chat bubble. It reads well in the pixel font because the dots are whole glyphs.

## 6. Reply All (front-runner, speed 80)

- Lets the whole field know. [26]
- **Ship:** Cc's the entire grandstand. [27]
- Never one to whisper. [21]

Why: office jargon, a fresh shape, and it pictures a front-runner out where everyone can see it. The grandstand is a generic word, not a real place.

## 7. Mostly Legs (closer, stamina 84, speed 58)

- **Ship:** The rest is mostly lungs. [25]
- Long stride, longer story. [26]
- Built like a folding ruler. [27]

Why: it finishes the name's sentence and lands on the horse's best stat. It is a compliment dressed as a punchline. It leaves out the low speed rather than making it the joke.

## 8. Mint Julep Lawsuit (stalker, stamina 82)

- **Ship:** Sweet, cold, and litigious. [27]
- Goes the distance. In court. [28]
- Garnished with mint. Sues. [26]

Why: a three-beat list that turns on the last word, a rhythm none of the other seven use. The second line nods at stamina, but "goes the distance" reads like a forecast, so I held it back.
